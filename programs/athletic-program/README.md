# Athletic Program: High-Low Model

## The different view

Most gym programs split the week by body part (upper/lower, push/pull). This one splits it by **how hard each day hits the nervous system**. That's the High-Low model Charlie Francis built for sprinters, and it still underpins a lot of field-sport programming.

- **HIGH days (Mon, Wed, Fri).** Everything that needs you fresh is done on the same day: sprinting, jumping, throwing and heavy lifting. Each HIGH day trains a different direction of movement.
- **LOW days (Tue, Thu, Sat).** Easy aerobic work, tendon health, structural/upper-body volume, carries and mobility. They're hard enough to build something and easy enough that you show up fresh for the next HIGH day.

You never stack two hard days back to back, and that's what makes 6 days sustainable. For a weekend athlete, the pitch is "3 hard days, 3 easy days, and Saturday can be your game."

| Day | Session | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | HIGH 1: Acceleration + Force | Linear. Sled/hill acceleration, clean or swing, Front Squat, Push Press, single-leg RDL, hamstring work |
| Tue | LOW 1: Tempo + Upper Structure | Tempo runs (65-70% speed), ring rows, push-ups, Y-raises, rotator cuff, bear crawls |
| Wed | HIGH 2: Lateral + Rotational | Frontal/transverse plane. Lateral bounds, rotational med ball, Cossack squat, chin-ups, single-arm press, Copenhagen plank |
| Thu | LOW 2: Aerobic Base + Tendon Work | Zone 2, Spanish squat and calf isometrics, tibialis raises, mobility flow |
| Fri | HIGH 3: Max Velocity + Max Strength | Flying sprints or build-ups, depth jumps or pogos, deadlift, incline press, seal row, cable chop |
| Sat | LOW 3: Conditioning | Weekend: your sport or Zone 2 + sled drags + carries. Off-season: extensive intervals + sled + carries |
| Sun | Rest | |

## Two tracks, same structure

- **`weekend-athlete-block-1-weeks-1-4.csv`.** HIGH days run about 60 min and LOW days 30-45 min.
  - Lower-risk options throughout: sled pushes instead of resisted sprints, KB swings instead of cleans, skater hops instead of max bounds, pogos instead of depth jumps, slider curls instead of Nordics, and sumo instead of conventional deadlift.
  - Saturday is the game.
- **`off-season-athlete-block-1-weeks-1-4.csv`.** Full-intensity speed and plyometrics, Olympic-lift derivatives, more sets on the main lifts, longer Zone 2, and a Saturday extensive-interval session.

## What's new compared with the old program

- **Training is split by direction, not body part.** There's a linear day, a lateral/rotational day and a max-velocity day. Sport happens in all three planes, and the old program lived almost entirely in one.
- **Tendon work.** Isometrics (Spanish squat, bent-knee calf holds) and tibialis raises are on the LOW days. Tendons adapt more slowly than muscle, and they're what break down when training load jumps.
- **Tempo runs instead of only intervals.** Low-intensity running volume builds aerobic recovery and running mechanics without adding to fatigue.
- **New main lifts:** Front Squat, Cossack/Lateral Squat, Deadlift, Push Press, Weighted Chin-up, Incline Press.

## Progression (one lever per week)

1. **Week 1, baseline.** Main lifts at RIR 3.
2. **Week 2, volume.** +1 set on main and secondary lifts. Tempo and Zone 2 volume go up.
3. **Week 3, load.** Add weight and drop a rep. Weekend track peaks at RIR 2, athlete track at RIR 1-2 (ACSM 2026: no need to train to failure).
4. **Week 4, deload.** About 40-50% less volume. Every session stays in, just lighter.

Speed and plyometric volume stays flat all block. You progress it by moving faster and jumping further, not by doing more reps.
