What goes in your gym bag

Essentials

  • Water bottle. Sip between sets, don't chug.
  • Small towel. For you and for wiping down the bench.
  • Stable, flat shoes. Cross trainers or flat soled shoes. Thick, squishy running shoes make you wobble on leg press and squats.
  • A supportive sports bra and clothes you can move in without adjusting every set.
  • Your program on your phone, plus a way to log weights (notes app or a training app).

Nice to have

  • Headphones. A good playlist helps, and they politely signal you're mid session.
  • Padlock for the locker.
  • Hair ties, deodorant, a light layer for warming up or a cold gym.
  • A snack or protein shake if you train straight after work and the next meal is hours away.

Skip the gloves, belts and supplements for now. You don't need them at this stage.

Gym etiquette (the unwritten rules)

Most people feel nervous in a new gym because nobody tells them the rules. Here they are. Follow these and you'll fit in from day one.

Wipe it downSpray and wipe any seat or pad you've sweated on when you finish.
Put it backDumbbells go back where they came from, pins come out of machines, plates come off.
Don't campRest on a machine between your sets, sure. Scrolling on it for ten minutes, no.
Ask to share"How many sets have you got left?" or "Mind if I jump in?" is completely normal.
Give people spaceDon't stand right in front of someone's mirror or walk through their set.
Headphones in means busyWait until they've finished a set if you need to ask something.
Ask the staffNot sure how a machine adjusts? Ask. That's what they're there for.
Nobody's watching youHonestly. Everyone is focused on their own session, and everyone started as a beginner.

Setting up any machine in 3 checks

1. Line the joint that's moving (knee, elbow, shoulder) up with the machine's pivot point, usually marked with a coloured dot. 2. Adjust the seat so the handles or pads sit where you start the movement comfortably. 3. Write the seat number in your log so next time takes ten seconds.

How you'll train, and why it works

Two or three full body sessions a week, mostly machines with a few easy dumbbell moves. Machines guide the movement path, so you can focus on effort and control while your body learns to produce force. Full body means every major muscle gets trained at least twice a week, which is exactly what the 2026 American College of Sports Medicine position stand on resistance training recommends, whatever your goal. Miss a session and nothing goes untrained for a week.

Sets: why 2–3

More total work builds more muscle, up to a point. The ACSM position stand found strength responds best to 2–3 hard sets per exercise. For a beginner that's the sweet spot. Enough to drive change, not so much you're wrecked for days. Start with 2 sets in weeks 1–2 while you learn the movements, then move to 3.

Reps: why 8–12

This range builds strength and muscle at the same time, and it's light enough to practise good technique with. Muscle can be built across a wide rep range (Schoenfeld and colleagues have shown this repeatedly), so 8–12 isn't magic, just the most practical place to start. Early on, most of your strength gains come from your nervous system getting better at the movement. That's why beginners get stronger so quickly, and why heavier, lower rep work can wait a few months.

Effort: why stop 2–3 reps short

This is the one most beginners get wrong, usually by stopping way too early. Every set should end feeling like you could have done 2 or 3 more good reps, no more. If you could have done 8 more, it was a warm up. You also don't need to grind to total failure. The 2026 ACSM review of 137 systematic reviews found training to failure gave no extra benefit for strength or muscle over stopping 2–3 reps short, just more fatigue. Hard, but clean.

Rest: why it isn't a race

Rest is when your muscles recover enough to do the next set properly. Cut it too short and your reps drop, which means less work done and less result. Take about 2 minutes on the big moves (leg press, goblet squat, Romanian deadlift) and 60–90 seconds on the smaller ones. Getting leaner comes from your food and your total weekly activity, not from rushing between sets until you're gasping.

Tempo: control the way down

Lower the weight over about 2–3 seconds, then push it back up with intent. Controlled lowering keeps tension on the muscle and the weight moving where it should, which protects your joints.

Your week and how to progress

2 days a week

Session A and Session B (next page), at least one rest day apart. Monday A, Thursday B works well.

3 days a week

Alternate them. Week 1 is A, B, A. Week 2 is B, A, B. For example Monday, Wednesday, Friday.

Getting stronger is the whole game. When you hit the top of the rep range on every set (say 3 × 12) and still had 2 reps left, go up to the next weight next session. Your reps will drop back toward 8. Build them back up to 12, then go up again. Log every session so you always know what to beat. A little more weight or one more rep every week or two adds up to a completely different body in three months.

"Will lifting make me bulky?" No. Building muscle is a slow process that takes years of deliberate effort and eating for it. You'll notice more shape and firmer muscle. The 2026 NSCA position statement on female athletes is clear that the same training principles apply to women as to men. You don't need a "pink dumbbell" version of training. You need the real thing, done well.

6

What happens outside the gym

Leaner

Fat loss is mostly driven by food. Training helps make sure the weight you lose is fat, not muscle. Aim for protein at every meal (a palm sized serve or more) and roughly 7,000–10,000 steps a day.

Recovery

Muscle is built while you recover, not while you train. Aim for 7–9 hours of sleep. Sore for a day or two early on is normal. Sharp or joint pain isn't, so stop that exercise and ask.

After 12 weeks. If you've logged your sessions and the weights have gone up, you're ready for more structure, heavier work and a smarter split. That's what the Female Specific Program is built for: strength and shape, programmed week by week. Until then, show up, log it and beat last week.
