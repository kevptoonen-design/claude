1

The goal and the approach

Ten kilos to lose, keto is staying, and protein is going up. That's a good combination, not a compromise. Keto's real value in a cut isn't some metabolic trick, it's appetite control. Higher fat and protein blunt hunger far better than a carb heavy diet does at the same calories, which is what actually determines whether a deficit gets followed for sixteen weeks instead of two. The deficit and the protein number are doing the work here. Ketosis itself doesn't burn extra fat on its own.

Pushing protein to around 210g a day inside a keto framework is sometimes called a high protein ketogenic diet, and it holds up in the research: higher protein increases satiety, raises the small metabolic cost of digesting food, and protects lean mass while you're eating below maintenance. That last point matters most at 95kg. The goal is to arrive at 85kg carrying the muscle you have now, not a smaller, softer version of the same weight loss.

Starting numbers

These are a starting point built off your current weight, not a fixed rule. Section 7 covers exactly how and when to adjust them.

Protein at 210g works out to roughly 2.2g per kilo of your current bodyweight, the higher end of what's useful in a deficit, and it's the number to defend hardest on a bad day. Carbs stay capped at 25g or under, mostly from vegetables, to keep you in ketosis. Fat fills the rest of the calories and is the lever you adjust if the scale stalls, not protein and not the carb cap.

Off a rough estimate of your maintenance intake at 95kg, 2,300 kcal sits around a 500 to 700 kcal daily deficit. That lines up with roughly 0.5 to 0.7kg lost a week, which puts the 10kg target somewhere around 14 to 20 weeks depending on adherence and how your body responds. That's an estimate to monitor, not a promise.

A day that hits the numbers

Build your day around three solid meals plus a protein top up if training lands late. Move the timing to fit your schedule, the structure is what matters.

Meal 1
4 whole eggs, 2 rashers bacon, spinach in butterProtein and fat forward start, near enough zero carb, keeps you full into the afternoon.
Meal 2
200g grilled chicken thigh, big salad, olive oil, half an avocadoThe biggest single protein hit of the day, fat comes from real food, not a bottle of dressing.
Meal 3
200–250g beef mince or salmon, roasted broccoli or cauliflower, butter or oilSecond big protein hit, carb from the veg only, this is where most of the day's fat can flex up or down.
Top up
Whey or casein shake, or a handful of macadamias/almondsUse this to close the gap to 210g protein on days meal 2 or 3 come up short, or to fill remaining fat calories with nuts.
4

Budget staples

Keto runs a bit more expensive than a rice and chicken plan because fat sources replace cheap carb fillers. Build around these and it stays manageable:

  • Eggs, chicken thighs, beef mince
  • Canned tuna and salmon
  • Butter, ghee, olive oil
  • Block cheese
  • Frozen broccoli, cauliflower, spinach
  • Avocado, when it's on sale buy several
  • Macadamias or almonds
  • Whey or casein protein powder
5

Eating out and the weekend fix

Order protein and fat, skip the rest Steak, chicken, or fish with a salad or non-starchy veg, sauce and dressing on the side. Skip the bun, the rice, the pasta, and the chips, that's where a "safe looking" meal quietly adds forty grams of carbs. If alcohol is part of the night, spirits with soda water or a dry wine in real moderation, beer and cocktails are carb heavy and alcohol hits harder without the carbs to buffer it. One planned flexible meal a week is fine. Trying to eliminate eating out completely is usually what causes the whole plan to fall apart by week three.
6

Keto specific watch outs

  • Electrolytes in week one and two: headaches, fatigue, and light headedness in the first fortnight are usually sodium, potassium, and magnesium leaving with the water your body's dropping, not a sign keto isn't working. Salt your food properly, don't go easy on it.
  • Fibre doesn't take care of itself: low carb often means low fibre by accident. Keep the leafy greens and cruciferous veg in every day, add a tablespoon of chia or psyllium if things get sluggish.
  • Ketone testing is optional, not required: the scale and the tape measure are the metrics that matter. Strips or a meter are fine if you want the data, but chasing a specific ketone number isn't the goal, fat loss is.
  • A stall isn't a signal to cut carbs to zero: the fat number moves first if progress stalls for two to three weeks straight. Protein and the carb cap don't move.
7

Timeline and how to adjust

Monitoring Weigh in 3x a week under the same conditions, track the weekly average rather than any single reading. Waist measurement every two weeks. Photos every two to four weeks.

Reassess every two to three weeks. If the weekly average and waist aren't trending down, drop fat by 15 to 20g a day, protein and the carb cap stay fixed. If you're losing faster than around 1% of bodyweight a week or strength in the gym is dropping off noticeably, add 15 to 20g of fat back in, that's muscle going with the fat, not extra progress.
