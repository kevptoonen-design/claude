The goal & the approach

You've been eating in an active bulk at ~3,800 kcal/day. The goal now is different: build muscle and bring body fat down — a recomposition, not a bulk. With your training already in place (5x/week, 8–12 rep hypertrophy work), that surplus needs to come down into a modest deficit, not stay in a surplus. Protein goes up, not down, to protect the muscle you're building while calories drop.

5

The weekend takeout fix

Don't eliminate it — plan it Cutting Friday night takeout out completely is how adherence dies. Instead, hit protein through the day as normal and let takeout fill the remaining carbs/fat for that day. Lean toward grilled over fried, sauces on the side. One planned flexible meal a week is sustainable — total elimination usually isn't.
6

What's already working — don't touch these

  • Sleep (~8+ hours, 9–9:30pm to 5:30am): genuinely excellent, and doing real work for recovery and body composition — a bigger lever than most of the diet itself.
  • Alcohol (next to none): removes a major variable that derails most recomps.
  • Sunday meal prep habit: the infrastructure is already there — this plan just changes what goes in it.
7

Timeline & how to adjust

Recomposition is slower than a pure bulk or pure cut — it's asking the body to do two things at once. Set expectations at 12–16+ weeks, not a fast transformation.

Monitoring Weigh in 3x/week (same conditions), track the weekly average. Waist measurement every 2 weeks. Photos every 2–4 weeks.

Reassess every 2–3 weeks: if weight/waist aren't trending down and lifts are holding steady, drop 150–200 kcal. If losing faster than ~0.5–1% bodyweight/week or strength starts dropping, add calories back — that's muscle loss, not fat loss.
