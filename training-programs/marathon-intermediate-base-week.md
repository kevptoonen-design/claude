# Marathon — Intermediate — Base/Build Week (v1)

**Athlete:** Intermediate marathon runner
**Phase:** Base/build (not taper, not peak race-specific week)
**Structure:** 4 run sessions, 2 strength sessions, 1 dedicated plyometric session, 1 rest day

This is a first draft — built to be argued with. Change volumes, swap days, tell me what doesn't fit the athlete's real schedule.

---

## The logic (read this before the sessions)

**From an S&C coaching lens:** concurrent training research (Rønnestad & Mujika) is clear — endurance athletes get the strength benefit from *low-volume, high-intensity* lifting, not bodybuilding-style volume. Two sessions a week, near-maximal loads, short-to-moderate rep ranges, full recovery between sets. More than that just adds fatigue that bleeds into running quality. Strength adaptation itself runs on **Henneman's size principle** — high-threshold motor units only get recruited under near-maximal load, so we're not chasing a pump, we're chasing neural drive.

**From a physio/sports doctor lens:** the plyometric session is quarantined to one day, done fresh, low ground-contact volume. Plyos and interference with running are a real risk if you stack them on tired legs — so this session goes on a day with no hard run before or after it. Purpose isn't "explosiveness" for its own sake — it's tendon stiffness and stretch-shortening cycle efficiency, which is what actually improves running economy (Saunders et al.; Ramírez-Campillo meta-analyses).

**Aerobic side runs on Seiler's polarized model:** ~80% of running volume stays in Zone 2 (60–70% max HR), ~20% sits at threshold/VO2max. No junk-pace running in between — either easy enough to talk in full sentences, or hard enough to mean it.

**SAID principle underwrites the whole week:** every session has a specific job. Nothing is in the plan "because it's good for you in general."

## The week

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Mon | Rest / mobility |
| Tue | **Strength A** — Lower-body max strength |
| Wed | **Threshold / VO2max run** |
| Thu | Easy run + **Strength B** — Upper body & core |
| Fri | **Dedicated plyometric session** |
| Sat | Easy run (short shakeout) |
| Sun | **Long run** |

---

### Tuesday — Strength A: Lower-Body Max Strength
*Goal: neural strength, not size. Near-maximal loads, low volume, full recovery. RIR 2–3 — never grind to failure this close to a hard run.*

1. Back Squat or Trap Bar Deadlift — 4×4 @ ~85% 1RM, 3 min rest
2. Single-Leg RDL — 3×6/leg, 90 s rest
3. Bulgarian Split Squat — 3×5/leg, 2 min rest
4. Straight-Leg Calf Raise — 4×8, heavy, slow eccentric (tendon stiffness → running economy)
5. Copenhagen Plank — 3×30 s/side (adductor/hip resilience — physio call, injury-prevention lens)

~45 min total.

---

### Wednesday — Threshold / VO2max Run
*The ~20% in Seiler's model. Alternate threshold-style and VO2max-interval weeks across the block.*

- 15 min warm-up, Zone 2
- 4 × 6 min @ threshold pace (comfortably-hard, sustainable ~60 min race effort), 90 s jog recovery
- 15 min cool-down, Zone 2

~55–60 min total.

---

### Thursday — Easy Run + Strength B: Upper Body & Core
*Legs stay easy — this is a Zone 2 day. Upper body/core work doesn't touch leg freshness for Sunday's long run.*

**Run:** 40 min, Zone 2 (60–70% max HR), conversational pace.

**Strength B:**
1. Pull-up or Lat Pulldown — 3×6–8
2. DB Bench Press or Push-up — 3×8
3. Single-Arm DB Row — 3×8/side
4. Pallof Press — 3×10/side (anti-rotation — carries directly into running posture under fatigue)
5. Dead Bug — 3×10

~30 min, moderate load, not to failure.

---

### Friday — Dedicated Plyometric Session
*Fresh legs only. No hard run within 24h either side. Quality over quantity — total ground contacts stay low (~80–100 for the session). This is not a conditioning session, it's a tendon-stiffness/SSC-efficiency session.*

Warm-up: 10 min dynamic + 2×10 pogo hops + 2×10 ankle skips

1. Pogo Jumps — 3×10, full recovery between sets
2. Countermovement Jumps — 3×5, max height, 90 s rest
3. Bounding — 4 × 10–15 m, walk back
4. Single-Leg Hop for Distance — 3×5/leg
5. Low Box Depth Jumps (~20–30 cm) — 3×5 *(only once athlete has a squat strength base — coach discretion)*

~25–30 min total, including warm-up.

---

### Saturday — Easy Run
30–40 min, Zone 2. Shakeout, primes legs for Sunday. Nothing to prove here.

---

### Sunday — Long Run
90–120 min, Zone 2 (majority of the week's aerobic volume lands here). Later in the build phase, add a final 15–20 min at marathon pace for specificity — not yet, in a base week.

---

## Open questions to tune this
- What's the athlete's actual current weekly mileage and long-run length? This assumes a moderate-volume intermediate base — needs calibrating to their real numbers.
- Preferred long-run day — Sunday assumed, adjust if their week runs differently.
- 1RM or working-weight benchmarks for the lifts, so Tuesday's % targets are real numbers, not placeholders.
- Any current niggles/injury history the physio lens needs to account for before locking the plyo session in.
